Getting Paid

How to Follow Up on Overdue Invoices Without Damaging the Relationship

By the StampBook team · Updated October 2026

Chasing money is uncomfortable, which is why many small business owners avoid it for weeks. But the longer an invoice sits, the less likely it is to be paid quickly. A calm, repeatable process makes follow-ups easy and keeps relationships intact.

First, check your own side

Before you send a reminder, confirm that the invoice reached the right person, that the amount and details are correct and that there is no pending dispute. Often the invoice is simply stuck with someone who is not the payer.

A five-stage reminder schedule

  1. Two days before the due date: a friendly heads-up.
  2. On the due date: a short note with the payment link.
  3. 3 to 5 days late: a polite check that nothing is blocking payment.
  4. 10 to 14 days late: a firmer message, a phone call and a clear new deadline.
  5. 30 days late: a formal notice, pausing new work until the dues are cleared.

Message templates

Before the due date

"Hi Meera, just a reminder that invoice SB-1088 (Rs. 18,000) is due this Friday. Here is the link to view and pay: [link]. Thank you!"

A few days late

"Hi Meera, I noticed invoice SB-1088 is still open. Could you let me know if you need anything from my side to process it? Happy to resend the details."

Firm follow-up

"Hi Meera, invoice SB-1088 is now 12 days overdue. Could you confirm the payment date by Monday? I would like to avoid pausing the next batch of work."

Formal notice

"This is a final reminder for invoice SB-1088, Rs. 18,000, overdue since 12 October. Please clear the payment by 31 October. After this date we will need to apply the late fee agreed in our terms and pause further work."

Choose the right channel

WhatsApp works for small businesses and freelancers because it is read quickly. Use email for corporate clients and keep a written record. A phone call is best for large amounts or repeated delays, followed by a written summary of what was agreed.

Make payment easy again

Every reminder should include the invoice link, the amount and your payment options. If paying takes more than two taps, you will wait longer.

When a client genuinely cannot pay

Offer a short payment plan in writing, for example two or three instalments with dates. Receiving something on a schedule is better than nothing, and it protects the relationship.

Prevent the problem next time

  • Take an advance from new clients.
  • State payment terms on every quotation and invoice.
  • Send invoices on the day of delivery, not weeks later.
  • Stop work on a client with a large unpaid balance.

Legal options as a last resort

If a client still does not pay, a legal notice from a lawyer is the usual next step. Micro and small enterprises also have a statutory route through the MSME Samadhaan portal and Facilitation Councils. Get proper legal advice before taking this path.
