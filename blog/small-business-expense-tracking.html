Expense Tracking for Small Businesses: A Simple System That Takes 10 Minutes a Week

By the StampBook team · Updated October 2026

Revenue is exciting, but profit is what you actually keep. Without tracking expenses you cannot know your real profit, your tax liability or whether a client is worth the effort. The good news is that a basic system takes only a few minutes a week.

Why tracking matters

  • It shows your true profit, not just money coming in.
  • It makes income tax and GST filing faster and less stressful.
  • It reveals subscriptions and costs that quietly drain cash.
  • It helps when applying for a loan, since lenders want to see records.

Step 1: Separate business and personal money

Open a dedicated bank account, and use one UPI ID for business. When everything is mixed, you will spend hours every year sorting transactions that should have been obvious.

Step 2: Choose a handful of categories

Too many categories make you give up. A simple set works for most small businesses:

CategoryExamples
Software and toolsDesign apps, hosting, domain, accounting software
TravelClient visits, fuel, transport
Office and suppliesStationery, equipment, internet, rent
MarketingAds, printing, website costs
Professional feesAccountant, legal help, freelancers you hire
OtherAnything that does not fit above

Step 3: Record expenses as they happen

Enter each expense with the date, amount, category and a short note. Photograph paper bills and keep them in one folder. If you pay on a card or UPI, the statement is a backup, but a note saying what the payment was for will save you months later.

Step 4: Keep GST bills if you claim input credit

If you are GST registered, you can generally claim credit on tax paid for business purchases, but you need a valid tax invoice in your name with your GSTIN. Ask suppliers to put your GSTIN on the bill at the time of purchase.

Step 5: Do a ten-minute weekly review

Pick a fixed time each week, such as Sunday evening. Add anything missing, check that unpaid invoices are followed up and glance at your total spending for the month. Small, regular habits beat a stressful annual cleanup.

Step 6: Review monthly

Once a month, compare income against expenses. Look for the largest categories, any subscription you no longer use and clients whose work costs more than it earns.

What to hand your accountant

  • A list of invoices issued and payments received
  • An expense list by category
  • Copies of key bills and bank statements
  • Details of any asset purchases

Having this ready lets your accountant work faster, which usually means a lower fee.

Tax treatment of expenses varies. Confirm what is deductible for your business with a qualified accountant.
